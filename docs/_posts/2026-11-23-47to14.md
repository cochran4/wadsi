## How We Selected the Factors

We started with 47 candidate factors previously examined in the Study to Explore Early Development (SEED).

The selection process included:

- **Scientific review:** Researchers evaluated scientific plausibility, public health relevance, developmental timing, data quality, and potential roles in the analysis.

- **Community Advisory Board (CAB):** CAB members also prioritized factors based on their knowledge and lived experiences. Across four meetings, researchers and CAB members discussed their assessments, including areas where their priorities differed.

- **Final selection:** These discussions helped identify 14 factors—nine for the primary model and five additional factors for the secondary model.

## Broadening Community Input

Before proceeding with the analysis, we held an Open Forum to hear from people beyond the CAB.

The forum provided an opportunity to:

- Bring in additional community perspectives.
- Raise questions or concerns about the selected factors.
- Reconsider selections if new information warranted changes.

The 14 factors remained unchanged, but the discussion added valuable context for understanding their relevance to the autism community.

## Why This Matters

Choosing which factors to study is more than a technical decision. These choices shape the questions researchers can answer and how findings are interpreted.

Bringing scientific and community perspectives together allows different forms of knowledge to inform these decisions, including when perspectives differ.

Our goal is not simply to include community perspectives in research, but to create meaningful opportunities for those perspectives to inform the decisions made throughout the research process.
